According to [Glitchwire's 10-18 report](https://glitchwire.com/news/odyssey-drops-a-fruit-fly-brain-into-its-world-model-and-lets-it-fly/), Odyssey did not say whether it used the FlyWire connectome, the MaleCNS dataset, or a derivative, nor how frames generated by Odyssey-3 become sensory input to the neural simulation; the report also notes that using the world model as a physics engine, feeding generated frames into the fly brain and returning motor signals, is an interpretation of what appears to have been done, not a company disclosure. On neuron count, the official post gives no figure; 166,000 matches the [complete male fruit fly central nervous system connectome published by Google Research and HHMI Janelia](https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/) (over 166,000 neurons and 125 million synapses, including the ventral nerve cord), while the 2024 FlyWire whole-brain map has 139,255 neurons (about 140,000, excluding the nerve cord). - Take: Visually striking, but only a video exists; the dataset version, neuron model type, and sensory-motor interface — the key details — are missing. It should be read as a demo that world models can host embodied agents, not as a reproducible scientific result.

### Google open-sources Harness²: agents rewrite their own harness, weights unchanged

- What happened: Researchers at Google Cloud AI Research and Northwestern University released Harness²: the harness around a model (eight components — system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts, plugins) is treated as a search space. Task-level recursion uses a contrastive Propose–Probe–Compose step to edit the current task's harness without ground-truth answer keys, and harnessing-level recursion stores each task's adopted harness plus evidence-supported lessons in a continual improvement harness for the next task; model weights never change.
- Why it matters: This is the most widely spread research post in the English technical circle this window (326K views). It turns "tuning the prompt, the tools, the memory" from manual trial and error into a layer that can be searched automatically, and it shows the same model scoring higher without retraining.
- Core posts: [@akshay_pachaar](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar/status/2111753928031531232) (10-18 17:39, 326K views, 4.2K likes, 512 reposts); Japanese explainer [@LangChainJP](https://x.com/LangChainJP/status/2111653664930583030) (844 views); Chinese [@aigclink](https://x.com/aigclink/status/2111789021290430767) (6.6K views, 68 likes); clarification [@riabcevv](https://x.com/riabcevv/status/2111906410996900102) (219 views).
- Verification: Checked against [arXiv 2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) (submitted 10-09) and the [google-research/harness2](https://github.com/google-research/harness2) repository. Confirmed: nine authors, affiliated with Google Cloud AI Research and Northwestern University; three professional benchmarks and four model-harness configurations, with Gemini 3.5 Flash and GLM-5 as frozen executors running under the off-the-shelf OpenCode and Codex harnesses; the default harness editor is Claude Opus 4.8 running under OpenCode; a single step (k=1) yields gains of up to 11.5 points on WorkBuddy-Bench, 4.5 points on JobBench, and 11.8 percentage points on LAB's partial pass rate, with further gains as the task-level recursion budget increases (the paper uses k=5). In the paper's tables, GLM-5 under OpenCode on WorkBuddy-Bench goes from a 64.2 to a 75.7 average, past untuned Gemini 3.5 Flash at 72.4. The repo is Apache 2.0 and the README states plainly that this is "not an officially supported Google product"; it documents the eight editable components, the OpenCode/Codex executors, and reproduction commands for the three benchmarks. The repo went up on October 6 and the paper was posted on October 9, so the "just open-sourced" framing on X is secondhand spread roughly ten days later. Two caveats: "no grader needed" refers to edit decisions not depending on answer keys, while benchmark evaluation still uses graders; and the README warns that executor network access is unrestricted and public benchmark answers may be reachable online, which must be restricted before evaluating — that affects how the scores should be read.
- Take: The direction is to treat the agent's workbench as an optimizable object; the benefit is that a weaker model can catch a stronger one by swapping harnesses. The cost is that every task runs several candidate versions, so cost rises with the recursion budget — in practice this is more likely to be an offline harness-tuning step with online reuse.

### vLLM builds a new decode path for Kimi K3 on GB300 NVL72, releases v0.32.0 the same day

- What happened: On 10-18 at 09:55 vLLM posted about a new decode path for Kimi K3 on NVIDIA GB300 NVL72: a unified CuTe DSL path fusing attention, MoE, and communication, hitting 842 tokens/s at batch 1, 1.5–2× higher decode throughput in several tested configurations, and up to 1.8× higher P90 interactivity on the SemiAnalysis AgentX workload. Coding agents helped build the path, and the write-up says outright that agent-optimized inference code is outpacing their review and maintenance workflow. The same day vLLM released v0.32.0 (814 commits, 363 contributors, 121 first-timers), adding a /v1/systemone structured-decision endpoint, low-precision KV paths (NVFP4, FP8, 4-bit), Model Runner V2, and support for EmbeddingGemma 2, MiniCPM-V 4.7, and Nemotron 3.5 ASR.
- Why it matters: This is a concrete case of "agents writing GPU kernels" moving from demo to production inference stack, and it puts the organizational question — how AI-written performance code gets reviewed — on the table.
- Core posts: [Kimi K3 decode post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2111637219647840611) (10-18 09:55, 36K views, 259 likes, 29 reposts); [v0.32.0 release post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2111814711956168760) (10-18 21:40, 11K views, 254 likes); paraphrase [@Cisco_research](https://x.com/Cisco_research/status/2111827196805333038) (126 views).
- Verification: Checked against the [vLLM blog post](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-16-kimi-k3-megakernel) (published 10-16, before the window; the X post is secondary spread). Confirmed the exact conditions for 842 tokens/s: 16 GB300 GPUs, TP16, DSpark with seven draft tokens, synthetic acceptance length AL=6, batch size 1, versus 488 tokens/s for stock vLLM, a 1.73× speedup; "1.5–2×" is the range across several batch and parallelism configurations; P90 interactivity on AgentX at concurrency 1 is 403.2 versus 223.7 tokens/s/user, i.e. 1.8× (note the original compares the optimized 16-GPU run with the earlier 8-GPU vLLM result); kernels per layer drop from 23 at 130 microseconds to 13 at 69 microseconds. A separate claim circulating of "423 tokens/s/user, 1.68×" does not match the blog's 403.2/223.7 and is treated as a source claim pending verification. Cisco_research's paraphrase that "agent-written GPU code is arriving faster than humans can review it" is close to the blog's own wording, but the blog does not argue for dropping review; it proposes a fast-track optimization RFC and automated validation to cut review burden.
- Take: The real story is a change in the engineering process — performance optimization now comes from agents, and human work shifts from reading every kernel line to designing automated verification and risk isolation; all the numbers are vendor self-tests under controlled conditions and do not transfer directly to production clusters.

### Four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft in 27 hours 14 minutes

- What happened: DeepMind researchers placed four Gemini 4 Argon agents in a sandboxed Minecraft survival world with a single goal: defeat the Ender Dragon. Each agent controlled a player via Mineflayer and coordinated only through in-game chat and shared memory; nobody assigned roles or wrote game logic. The team wrote 2,783 programs and beat the dragon in 27 hours 14 minutes, dying 605 times along the way.
- Why it matters: This is a public case where long-horizon work, multi-agent coordination, and autonomous coding all hold at once. Within the window a DeepMind researcher posted congratulations and amplifier accounts relayed it, but the paper itself predates the window.
- Core posts: DeepMind's [@RaiaHadsell congratulations post](https://x.com/RaiaHadsell/status/2111884126324490377) (10-19 02:16, 747 views, 12 likes); representative spread [@RoundtableSpace](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2111815813842026866) (42K views, 44 likes); The Decoder citation [@Chahatusharma](https://x.com/Chahatusharma/status/2111800220384378893) (88 views).
- Verification: Checked against [arXiv 2610.22319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319) (submitted 10-15). Confirmed: a randomly generated world on Easy difficulty, no pre-scouted seed, no human-written game-specific tools; 2,783 programs, 27 hours 14 minutes, 605 deaths; researchers could watch a live dashboard and pause or stop the run at any time but had no cause to intervene. The claim of "the first end-to-end autonomous completion of Minecraft by AI agents" is the authors' own (to our knowledge), not a third-party determination.
- Take: What matters is not that AI can play Minecraft, but that it held a plan across thousands of dependent steps for more than a day and recovered through division of labor, shared lessons, and retrieving each other's dropped items; Easy difficulty, a single world, cost, and failure rate are all in the paper's stated limits and do not translate directly into general engineering ability.

### Microsoft pulls out of Nscale's Loughton, UK data center project

- What happened: Per the Telegraph's 10-18 report, followed the same day by BBC and Bloomberg, Microsoft terminated its agreement with British AI cloud company Nscale for the Loughton site in Essex, where it had planned a supercomputer with more than 23,000 GPUs as anchor tenant. Grid operators told Nscale that power for the site is not expected until the 2030s; Nscale says it remains committed to the £2bn project, is exploring alternative power sources, and that its other work with Microsoft in Norway, Portugal, and the US is unaffected. Microsoft declined to comment.
- Why it matters: The demand story has run for a year; this is a concrete case of a hard supply-side constraint — not that chips cannot be bought, but that the site cannot be plugged in, and at a project billed as the UK's largest AI data centre.

- Verification: Checked against [BBC's report](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9m26kl5y7epo), confirming the termination, the wait until the 2030s for power, the £2bn investment, Nscale's statement that it will keep building, seek alternative power, and that other overseas projects with Microsoft are unaffected, and Microsoft's no-comment. The [Telegraph original](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2026/10/18/microsoft-axes-ai-deal-setback-uk-data-centre-pioneer/) and [Bloomberg's follow-up](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/microsoft-scraps-uk-data-center-deal-with-nscale-telegraph-says) are paywalled and only headlines and summaries were checked.
