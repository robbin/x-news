### OpenAI reports progress on COBOL, Fortran and Visual Basic, and open-sources MultiLangBench

- What happened: OpenAI posted on 10-20 at 06:30 that its latest models are stronger at building and migrating low-resource programming languages such as COBOL, Fortran and Visual Basic, with the focus on understanding decades of business logic and preserving existing behavior during modernization, and it invited enterprise customers to suggest the next language and workflow to tackle.
- Why it matters: this splits "writing code" into two questions that are closer to enterprise budgets: building from scratch (writing a SQLite-compatible engine in 20 languages) and migration (moving COBOL to C++, Java, Kotlin, Python, Rust with unchanged behavior). The latter maps directly onto the installed systems of banks, governments and insurers, and is also where AI coding has the hardest time earning production trust.
- Key posts: [@OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2112310345863930168) (10-20 06:30, 206K views, 2,478 likes, 169 reposts, 237 replies, 116 quotes).
- Verification: against the [OpenAI official blog post](https://openai.com/index/low-resource-programming-languages/) (published 10-19), the design and scores of both evaluations are confirmed: MultiLangBench requires models to build a SQLite-compatible database engine from scratch, using the database file as the cross-language interface; GPT-6 Astra averages an 87.4% test pass rate across 20 languages, while GPT-5.6 Sol is 26.8%; by language, COBOL rises from 10.9% to 72.1%, OCaml 25.6%→85.8%, Fortran 21.4%→83.5%, Visual Basic 27.5%→89.4%, with Astra above 83% on 19 of 20 languages, COBOL still the hardest. GPT-6 Luna averages 40.3%, with an estimated API cost of 0.77 dollars per sample versus 3.48 dollars for GPT-5.6 Sol (about 78% lower). The COBOL migration evaluation requires byte-identical output and all hidden tests passing to count as success: GPT-6.1 Sol at low reasoning effort scores 86.6%, GPT-5.6 Sol 5.4%, at about 61% of the latter's cost (the blog's opening writes "39% cheaper," which is consistent); at high reasoning effort it is 97.9%. The evaluation set and per-language results are in [openai/MultiLangBench](https://github.com/openai/MultiLangBench). The X post itself has no numbers; "800 billion lines of COBOL" comes from CryptoBriefing's coverage, not from OpenAI's own text, and is not cited here.
- Assessment: this treats "low-resource languages" as an independent capability dimension rather than waiting for general coding ability to spill over; COBOL migration uses hidden tests and byte-level consistency, close to real compliance requirements, which is more meaningful than "it compiles." But all scores are vendor self-reported, and OpenAI itself says production migration still needs expert review; what really matters is whether enterprises can use this evaluation to reproduce their own codebases, not the average pass rate.

### Meta publishes the Muse Glimmer technical report: 30B open weights, trained inside a "Sims-like" life

- What happened: Meta announced on 10-20 at 03:33 the release of the Muse Glimmer technical report. It is a 30B open-weight model aimed at agent tasks on consumer hardware, able to run on a single consumer GPU after 4-bit quantization, and the company says it leads same-size open models in tool use and multi-step planning. The report discloses the architecture, the distillation recipe from a larger model, quantization and speculative decoding.
- Why it matters: the model itself shipped back in August; what is new here is the technical report, and the value is in the training method: Meta trains the agent on "simulated digital lives" with 10–14 contacts and weeks of email, calendar, document and message history, and removes hallucinations, false successes and unverified trajectories from the data. That says more about how to build a "local personal agent" than another agent benchmark score.
- Key posts: [@AIatMeta](https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2112265822274302066) (10-20 03:33, 68K views, 721 likes, 91 reposts, 73 replies, 63 quotes); analysis [@kimmonismus](https://x.com/kimmonismus/status/2112270439078625477) (28K views, 241 likes); team member [@arreqe_ai](https://x.com/arreqe_ai/status/2112306216139685921) (303 views, 6 likes).
- Verification: against the [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B), it is confirmed as Apache 2.0, about 29.6B dense parameters (including about 1.8B vision encoder), 131,072+ context, knowledge cutoff 2026-01-04, runnable in 24GB / 32GB of VRAM; K-Quant-17GB on an RTX 5090 (Meta's own testing) reaches 74.9 tokens/s without speculative decoding and 233.4 tokens/s with DFlash speculative decoding (3.1 times), and 37.8 on M4 Max, 50.2 on M5 Max. Against the [Meta technical report page](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/muse-glimmer-30b-an-on-device-agentic-model/) and its PDF, section 6.2's synthetic data pipeline is confirmed: each "universe" is a pre-populated digital life containing 10–14 contacts, weeks of correspondence, calendar, documents and message history, with tasks spanning multiple days; post-processing removes failed, duplicate and fourth-wall-breaking items, plus any trajectory containing hallucinations, false successes or unverified results. kimmonismus's account matches the report. On independent evaluation, [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/muse-glimmer) gives Muse Glimmer 35 an Intelligence Index of 35, below Qwen3.6 27B's 38 and above Gemma 4 31B's 30; GDPval-AA v2 is only 953 Elo, below Qwen3.6 27B's 1141; AA-Omniscience hallucination rate is 82% versus Qwen3.6 27B's 49%. Meta's claim of "leading at the same size" mainly holds for tool calling and some agentic items, not across the board.
- Assessment: the interesting part of Muse Glimmer is not "30B can fight frontier models" but the engineering combination of "30B + quantization + speculative decoding + distillation from simulated environments" that puts an agent on local hardware. The weaknesses exposed by independent evaluation are also clear: weak knowledge work and calibration, high hallucination rate. It fits as a local execution layer, not as a final arbiter for decisions requiring highly reliable facts. The data-filtering rules in the technical report are more worth copying by other teams than the leaderboard rankings.

### Cloudflare releases Clairic: no generation, just typed values back

- What happened: Cloudflare released Clairic on 10-20 at 02:06, calling it a System One extraction model that can extract, compute, estimate and predict numeric values from unstructured text or images, in a single forward pass without generating tokens, outputting typed results with confidence / probability. It is already in preview on Workers AI as @cf/cloudflare/clairic, with open weights "soon."
- Why it matters: a large share of business calls are really just "put the amount, date and order number from this email into a table," and doing that with a generative LLM is both slow and billed by output token. Clairic turns these tasks into regression problems: no options to choose from, direct estimation of continuous values, plus p10–p90 intervals, which engineering-wise is closer to an auditable extractor.
- Key posts: [@Cloudflare](https://x.com/Cloudflare/status/2112244042658423149) (10-20 02:06, 53K views, 897 likes, 106 reposts, 27 replies, 44 quotes); [@michellechen](https://x.com/michellechen/status/2112241006104244584) (95K views, 1,005 likes); [@kevin_flansburg](https://x.com/kevin_flansburg/status/2112248553703928141) (32K views, 372 likes).
- Verification: against the [Cloudflare documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clairic/), it is confirmed as 65,536 token context, 0.10 dollars per million input tokens, no output token billing, support for text and up to 4 images, field types including text / number / date / list / estimate, returning confidence, p_null and intervals; requests can split fields across multiple parallel passes of 8,192 tokens. Against [PPC Land's item-by-item breakdown of Cloudflare's own testing](https://ppc.land/cloudflares-clairic-model-cuts-ai-pipeline-cost-49-in-its-own-test/), the official three-stage claims pipeline is confirmed: Clairic + Clef + GLM-5.3 end to end in 19.3 seconds, versus 61.7 seconds for GLM-5.3 throughout (about 3.2 times faster); cost per million claims 6,032 dollars versus 11,740 dollars (49% saved); extraction plus decision together 0.68 seconds, versus 38 seconds for GLM-5.3 on the same two steps. Note: the blog's "higher accuracy" has no general benchmark behind it, only one claims case and a 21-day forecasting test, with the number of runs undisclosed; the open weights have no date; kevin_flansburg says "based on regression rather than classification," while the blog frames it as System One / decision model without giving regression formula details.
- Assessment: this is a clear example of splitting decisions and extraction out of generative models. For invoices, claims, routing and risk control — businesses with fixed fields and high call volume — the cost and latency gains are real; but at preview stage, with a single pipeline self-test, the right move is shadow-traffic comparison first, not directly replacing production decisions.

## 2. AI compute and industry

### Meta discloses Prometheus: a million-GPU target, 85% goodput and a 99% cut in interruptions

- What happened: Meta disclosed details of Prometheus on 10-20 at 02:14 — a gigawatt-scale supercluster in New Albany, Ohio. The first phase is complete and has trained the Muse family of foundation models (including Muse Wonder); pretraining goodput exceeds 85% and reinforcement learning exceeds 90%, and during Muse Wonder training the mean time between interruptions was about 47K GPU hours, later raised above 54K through firmware fixes. The cluster is currently several hundred thousand GPUs across multiple buildings, targeting about 1 million, and will introduce Vera Rubin NVL72 on the San Miguel platform.
- Why it matters: this is a public lesson in translating "compute scale" into operational metrics. For very large training, one bad card, switch or link can stall an entire batch of jobs; 85% goodput means about 15% of effective compute is still eaten by failures, recovery and waiting. The 30–40% power transients on the electrical side — tens of megawatts swinging within tens of milliseconds — are problems other teams will hit sooner or later.
- Key posts: [@AIatMeta](https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2112245968750145561) (10-20 02:14, 147K views, 2,329 likes, 182 reposts, 109 replies, 63 quotes); [NVIDIA AI Infra](https://x.com/NVIDIAAIInfra/status/2112264876928422128) (5,074 views, 121 likes); detail thread [@choblin29](https://x.com/choblin29/status/2112275777375101105) (2,307 views, 24 likes).
- Verification: against the [Meta engineering blog](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/10/19/data-center-engineering/inside-prometheus-meta-gigawatt-scale-supercluster/), it is confirmed that the first phase is complete and has trained Muse Wonder, the target is about 1 million GPUs, and the current several hundred thousand GPUs span multiple buildings; 85%+ pretraining goodput, 90%+ RL goodput, about 47K GPU hours MTBI (later 54K+); in mid-2025 peaks approached 1,000 interruptions per day per 8,000 GPUs, cut 99% within a year; to keep 32,768 GPUs training continuously, it actually configures 523 NVL72 racks, 37,656 GPUs (about 13% headroom, of which 11% comes from the 64/72 slicing geometry); power transients of 30–40%, with Dynamo Dimmer doing cluster-level power capping alongside rack batteries, capacitor trays and energy storage; the network BAG spans 2–100 km. choblin29's details are basically accurate, but "plans to expand to about 1 million and add Vera Rubin" is information Meta had already made public, not his exclusive; NVIDIA's "cutting interruptions by 99%" matches Meta. A distinction is needed: 47K / 54K is MTBI (mean time between interruptions), not the same metric as "failure rate down 99%" — one looks at the interval, the other at the reduction.
- Assessment: these numbers show that once scale goes up, reliability engineering is itself part of model capability. Spare-part ratios, fault-domain size and power-transient management all turn directly into training cost and iteration speed. The lesson for peers is not "buy more cards" but to treat goodput and MTBI as first-class metrics from the start; 85% also reminds us that there is a considerable gap between nominal and effective compute.
