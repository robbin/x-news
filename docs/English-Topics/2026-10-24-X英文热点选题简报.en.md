### 15. Google DeepMind: every mainstream softmax and cross-entropy implementation has two numerical bugs

Original posts and traction: [@jon_barron](https://x.com/jon_barron/status/2113707875290042665) (10-24 03:03, post age 5.8h, within 24 hours, 132K views, 2.4K likes, 240 reposts, 50 replies, 1653 bookmarks, 38 quotes): "every existing softmax and cross-entropy implementation (JAX, PyTorch, textbooks, etc.) has a subtle bug, actually two. A simple rewrite fixes them." Primary source: [the paper arXiv 2610.31642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642) (10-22).

Content and angles: the two bugs are Softmax Collapse: when one logit is far larger than the others, the gradient of the target logit rounds to zero and the gradients no longer sum to zero; and Softmax Cancellation: during distillation, when student and teacher logits are close, a gradient that should be zero is not. The fix is to rewrite in log space with softplus, expm1 and log1p, which works in bf16, fp32 and fp64 without internal conversion to higher precision. The paper says this eliminates the "slingshot" phenomenon in grokking, and that grokking is faster with weight decay.

Why it is worth doing (possible angles): 1653 bookmarks is far above reposts, so this leans toward researchers and engineers saving it. For the owner's audience it could be a short explainer: "even softmax has bugs" is inherently shareable, but it should be clear that the impact is mainly in high-confidence training and distillation, not that existing models are all wrong.

Timeliness and verification: within 24 hours. The author, affiliation, the two bug definitions and the fix are verified (paper).
